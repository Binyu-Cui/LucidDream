## Explain your game elements and how to play your game, include images
**BACKGROUND**
- Misty Forest  
Players venture into a shrouded forest, where eerie sounds echo through the trees, and hidden treasures tempt the bold. Along the journey, mysterious cards are drawn, each granting unique powers.

- Forest Monsters
At the heart of the forest, a chaotic and powerful monster awaits. Only by uniting their strengths can players hope to survive its wrath and move forward.

- The Infection
The battle leaves one player tainted by an unknown force, their identity concealed. A hidden threat begins to unravel as fate takes its course.

- Duel of Fates
All alliances shatter in the final showdown. Players clash in a battle for survival, their collected cards shaping their destiny and the ultimate outcome of their journey.

**GAME RULES**
- Level 1 
- Players roll the dice to advance.
- Landing on a special space means drawing a random card.
- The power value of the card remains hidden until later levels.

- Level 2
- Players reveal three cards in their hand, but the values are unknown.
- Drawing cards generates the monster's health value.
- Cards are flipped to reveal their values. If the total card value exceeds the monster's health, the player advances. Otherwise, the level resets.

- Level 3
- Spin the wheel to determine which player is infected.

- Level 4
- Players take turns choosing cards to play.
- The card values determine the winner. If a player's card has a higher value, they beat the opponent's card and subtract that value in the next round.
- The duel continues until one player loses all their cards，
and the other player wins.

## These are the things it made me think about games in general:

The core concept behind "Choice of Fate" centered on creating what the course materials call "second-order design" - a system where individual rules, actions, and goals combine to generate emergent experiences beyond their individual components. Four distinct levels guide players through completely different social dynamics: forest exploration, monster battle, infection reveal, and final duel. Each level functions as its own bounded "playspace" with unique rules and temporal constraints, yet together they create a cohesive narrative system. Level 3's infection reveal serves as the critical turning point, deliberately designed to shatter previously established cooperation and transform allies into potential enemies. This transformation demonstrates how changing one part of a system affects the whole, creating predictable yet dramatic behavioral patterns.

Balancing randomness with strategic decision-making became essential to maintaining what the materials describe as meaningful "constraints" within the game's possibility space. While dice, card draws, and the spinner introduce genuine uncertainty, the design avoids pure luck-based outcomes. Level 2's monster battle exemplifies this approach - players must calculate whether their combined card values can overcome the monster's health, creating tension between uncontrollable random factors and deliberate strategic choices. This balance ensures that both "skill" and "chance" contribute to the experience, preventing either anxiety from impossible challenges or boredom from trivial decisions. The design maintains what flow theory calls the optimal challenge-to-skill ratio across all four levels.

The handmade approach emerged from understanding the visceral design principles outlined in the course materials - the immediate emotional impact of physical interaction. Decorated cards, hand-drawn boards, and a homemade spinner serve purposes beyond aesthetics; they function as what the materials call "objects" that players use to achieve goals. There's something fundamentally different about the tactile experience of touching cards and spinning a physical wheel compared to digital interactions. The weight, texture, and slight imperfections of handmade components create deeper behavioral engagement while establishing clear spatial and temporal boundaries for the play experience. This physicality anchors the game in real space and time, making the playspace tangible and memorable.

Trust and betrayal drive the game's emotional architecture, operating across all three design levels identified in the course materials. The visceral level emerges through immediate reactions to dice rolls and card reveals. The behavioral level develops through the strategic cooperation required in early levels, where players must genuinely help each other survive the forest and defeat the monster. The reflective level crystallizes in Level 3's infection mechanism, which deliberately fractures established social bonds and forces players to question everything that came before. This progression from cooperation to suspicion isn't accidental - it creates what the materials describe as "carnivalesque" play, balancing creation and destruction within the game's bounded duration. Through multiple playtests, this emotional journey has proven remarkably consistent, demonstrating how well-designed constraints can generate reliable yet surprising emergent behaviors in the system of player relationships.

## These are the ideas it gave me about games  
Instead of seeing them as entertainment products, I learned that games are systems that create their own bounded time and space with clear rules and endings. The concept that play can "take over" and transform social situations directly influenced my design approach - I realized that each level could deliberately shift what the interaction means for players. Understanding the balance between challenge and skill helped structure progression, while learning the difference between "play as activity" and "playfulness as attitude" showed me that designing games isn't just about making mechanics, but creating conditions where people willingly engage emotionally with each other.
