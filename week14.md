# Week 14

### Labs

The idea of the Metaverse is presented as a persistent, interconnected 3D space that should be open, decentralized, and hardware-agnostic—at least according to Tony Parisi’s rules. But I can’t help noticing the disconnect between this vision and what companies like Meta are actually building. When a single corporation controls the infrastructure, it directly contradicts the whole “no single controller” principle. It makes me wonder whether Parisi’s ideal is more of a manifesto than a feasible framework.

The second deck brings up embodiment in virtual environments and how we’re supposed to navigate this “split self”—our physical body in one world and our avatar in another. It makes sense that bodily experience shapes how we engage with virtual space, but I’m still unsure how far that really goes. If my avatar looks nothing like me, does that change how I feel or how others perceive me? There’s a tension here between identity and presence that doesn’t seem fully resolved.

Interoperability comes up a lot, and it’s clearly positioned as non-negotiable for a functioning Metaverse. But again, there’s a gap between theory and practice. On paper, assets should move smoothly between platforms. In reality, the ecosystem is fractured—Meta and Lamina1 follow completely different models, and I don’t see how they could ever be fully compatible. If every major player builds their own siloed space, the idea of a truly open network starts to fall apart.

The use cases listed—like immersive education or shared performance spaces—sound promising, but they remain pretty abstract. There’s not much detail about how any of this would actually work. It’s easy to say VR classrooms will be better than traditional ones, but without real examples, it’s just speculation. I need to see how these imagined benefits translate into something functional.

Then there’s the blockchain. Lamina1 puts a lot of weight on it as a tool for decentralization and user ownership. But even the materials admit there’s skepticism, especially around energy use and complexity. I’m not convinced it solves more problems than it creates. It feels like an answer in search of a question, or at least one that still needs better justification.

### Project Development
**1. Core Theme & Research Direction**  
Main Focus: The tension between self-identity and societal roles.  
Key Questions:  
- How does society shape human identity, especially during key life transitions?  
- What happens when one rejects traditional expectations—but remains unconsciously influenced by them?  
- Can the subconscious (through dreams) offer alternative futures or paths?  

**2. Narrative Structure & Method**  
Form: A crossing of dream and reality, using nonlinear time and extended worldbuilding.  
Temporal Strategy:   
- Time in dreams becomes disordered, reflecting the delayed processing of personal struggle.  
- Dreams act as metaphors for subconscious resistance, unfolding a parallel reality.  
- The narrative shifts between the protagonist’s orderly, socially accepted routine and their surreal, emotionally charged dream experiences.  

**3. Character Design & Internal Conflict**  
Protagonist: A student on the verge of graduation, potentially becoming a teacher—symbolizing both individual and institutional roles.  
Conflicts:  
- As a person: What should I choose?
- As a teacher: What am I expected to choose?
Torn between rationality and ethics, like a version of the trolley problem.  
Psychological State: A growing sense of dissociation, where identity becomes fragmented between inner desires and outer expectations.

**4. Worldbuilding & Expression of Ideas**  
Dream Layer:  
A representation of subconscious resistance—offering “non-traditional” paths.  
Society’s influence appears as embedded “settings” or invisible scripts that are difficult to rewrite.  
Resistance is expressed symbolically: silent movements, refusal to speak, abstract protest rituals.  

**5. Summary**  
Theme: Self-identity in conflict with societal norms  
Form: Blending dream and reality    
Social Context: A graduating student at a turning point, negotiating between personal value and public role
