# Week 16

### Labs

The PechaKucha format compels me to strip ideas down to their essence. With only 20 seconds per slide, I must eliminate filler and crystallize key messages, forcing clarity I wouldn’t achieve in freeform talks. The auto-advancing slides impose rigorous discipline, training me to synchronize speech with visual pacing—a skill that translates to tighter, more engaging presentations overall. Relying primarily on images rather than text pushes me toward visual storytelling, which not only holds audience attention better but also fosters creative communication. The strict 6-minute-40-second total duration respects listeners’ time while demanding impactful delivery.

However, the constraints introduce significant hurdles. Condensing complex concepts into 20-second segments risks oversimplification or loss of nuance, especially with technical subjects. Sourcing 20 high-impact visuals demands substantial time—far more than preparing text-heavy slides. Technical execution is precarious; a single auto-advance glitch or mistimed click could derail the entire flow. With minimal on-slide text as a safety net, I’m forced to memorize content thoroughly, heightening pressure during live delivery. The format leaves no room for improvisation or recovery from stumbles.

Ultimately, this rigidity is its greatest strength and weakness. It eradicates rambling and builds essential presentation muscles—concision, rhythm, visual thinking. Yet mastery requires dozens of rehearsals to internalize timing and weave narratives fluidly. For someone accustomed to detailed explanations, adapting to this brevity feels like learning a new language. The payoff is substantial, but the learning curve demands patience and tolerance for imperfection.
